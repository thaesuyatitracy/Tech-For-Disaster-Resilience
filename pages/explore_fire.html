Detecting Fire Before It Spreads

Wildfires spread quickly, damaging forests and communities. Modern tech helps detect unusual heat early and supports firefighting efforts.

Thermal Imaging Satellites

Thermal imaging satellites detect heat patterns on the Earth's surface.

  • Heat Detection: Identifies high temperatures.
  • Fire Location: Spots developing fires.
  • Large-Area Monitoring: Observes remote forests.

Fire Monitoring Drones

Drones equipped with cameras and thermal sensors can fly over areas affected by wildfires.

  • Aerial Observation: Captures images from above.
  • Thermal Cameras: Detects high temperatures.
  • Situation Monitoring: Observes changes in fires.

AI Fire Risk Prediction

Artificial Intelligence analyzes weather, vegetation, and historical data to predict fire risks.

  • Risk Mapping: Highlights high-danger zones.
  • Weather Analysis: Tracks dry and windy conditions.
  • Early Prevention: Helps teams prepare before fire starts.

Everyday Tech You Can Use

You don't need a drone to help prevent wildfires. Simple community tools and a little awareness can make a huge difference.

NASA FIRMS Fire Map

Try: NASA FIRMS Fire Map (Website).

Open the map and take a screenshot showing the active-fire markers. NASA explains that these are satellite-detected thermal anomalies, not confirmation that every marked location is an actual fire.

FireTrack Fiji | Community Fire Safety

Try: Explore the FireTrack Fiji Viber community.

FireTrack Fiji is a platform that helps communities stay informed about fire safety by sharing fire alerts, safety tips, prevention guidelines, and useful resources.

Stop Fire Before It Spreads

Wildfire Preparedness

Wildfires often begin with a single spark. Small, careful habits can prevent them, and save homes, forests, and lives.

Special Wildfire-Kit Items

N95 masks
Goggles / eye protection
Battery-powered radio
Small fire extinguisher
Long-sleeved cotton clothing
Emergency whistle

Habits to Practise

1

Never play with matches or lighters near dry grass.

2

Report any smoke or fire immediately to an adult.

3

Keep your home clear of dry leaves and rubbish.

4

Never try to fight a fire yourself, leave it to firefighters.

5

Remind adults not to burn rubbish on windy days.

Before, During & After a Wildfire

Before the Fire
1

Create a defensible space around your home (5 to 10 metres of cleared vegetation).

2

Check smoke alarms monthly and replace batteries yearly.

3

Agree on a family meeting point outside the danger zone.

4

Prepare a "go-bag" for each family member.

During the Fire
1

Evacuate immediately when authorities say to.

2

Stay low to avoid smoke; cover your nose with a damp cloth.

3

Follow official instructions, never re-enter your home.

After the Fire
1

Only return when authorities say it is safe.

2

Watch for hot spots, the ground can stay hot for hours.

3

Check on neighbours, especially elderly people.
